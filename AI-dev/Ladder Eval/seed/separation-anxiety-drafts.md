# Draft rungs for the situations Dr. Walker left blank

**Written 2026-08-31, by Claude, for her to correct.** These are drafts, not answers.

Eleven situations from her separation-anxiety file that arrived with no rungs. The moves used here
are the ones present in her own ladders — imagining it in detail, watching other people do it,
rehearsing it, changing who is involved, removing information, and a prerequisite where one
situation clearly has to come first. Duration where the situation can be shortened, and not where
it cannot.

Nothing here keeps a safety behaviour, and nothing has a way out built into it.

**Two of these overlap her existing entries** — "Attend school" sits close to her "School drop off"
and "First day of school". Flagged rather than merged, because which one survives is her call.

---

## Parents go to a party

- Imagine your parents at a party: you don't know who is there, when it will end, or how to reach
  them. Play it again like a film.
- Parents go to a neighbour's for half an hour with the sitter, and you do not contact them
- Parents go out for the evening and tell you when they will be back
- Parents go out for the evening and do not tell you when they will be back
- Parents go out and you cannot call or text, and neither can the sitter
- Parents come home after you are asleep

## Parents travel

*Probably after they can be out for an evening with no contact.*

- Imagine your parents on a plane, away for several days, and you cannot picture where they are
- One parent away for one night
- One parent away for three nights
- Both parents away for one night
- Both parents away for several nights
- Away with no arranged times to speak

## New sitter

- Imagine a sitter you have never met arriving at the door
- Meet the new sitter while your parents are at home
- The new sitter stays with you while a parent is in another room for ten minutes
- A parent goes out for half an hour with the new sitter there
- A parent goes out for the evening with the new sitter there
- A sitter you have not met before that evening

## New school

- Imagine your first day: not knowing anyone, not knowing where the rooms are, not knowing who to
  sit with at lunch
- Watch videos of children starting at a new school — not ones meant to be reassuring
- Walk around the building when it is empty, with a parent
- Visit while school is running, with a parent
- Walk in on your own while a parent waits in the car park
- A taster day on your own
- The first full day

## Parent late for pick up

*The lateness is the thing being faced, so it is practised on purpose.*

- Imagine standing at the gate after everyone else has gone, not knowing where your parent is
- A parent is five minutes late, arranged beforehand, and you do not call
- Fifteen minutes late
- Late with no warning
- Late, with no warning, and you cannot call or text

## Sleep over at a friend's house

*Probably after sleeping in your own room without the good-nights, check-ins and lights.*

- Your friend sleeps over at your house
- Imagine waking in the night in a house that is not yours
- Sleep over at your closest friend's house
- Sleep over there again, and again
- Sleep over with no calling or texting home
- Sleep over at the house of a friend you know less well
- Sleep over where there are other children too

## Go to summer camp

- Imagine the first morning: being dropped off, not knowing anyone, a day that is not planned by you
- Watch videos of children at camp
- Visit the camp with a parent before it starts
- A half day
- A full day
- A full week
- A camp where you know nobody

## Go to sleep away summer camp

*Probably after sleeping at a friend's house with no contact home.*

- Imagine a cabin of people you do not know, no phone, and no idea what your parents are doing
- Watch videos of children at sleep away camp
- One night away with relatives
- Two nights
- A weekend away
- A week, with letters only
- A full session with no calls

## Attend school

*Overlaps her "School drop off" and "First day of school" — hers may cover this.*

- Imagine a whole school day, from the gate to the end
- Go into the building after hours with a parent
- Go in for one lesson
- A half day
- A full day
- A full week
- A full day on a morning when you wake up anxious

## Attend sport games, lessons or training

- Imagine being dropped at training and your parent driving away, and nobody watching you
- Watch videos of children at training with no parents there
- A parent stays and watches the whole session
- A parent watches from the car park
- A parent drops you off and comes back at the end
- A parent drops you off and is late back
- A game with no family watching

## Go to college

*The far end of this ladder, and closer to a life stage than a week's exposure.*

- Imagine your first night in a room of your own, among people you have never met
- An overnight visit or open day, staying over
- A week away on a course or programme
- Move in with no arranged times to call home
- A full term without coming home
