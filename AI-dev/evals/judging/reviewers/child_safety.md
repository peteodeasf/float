# The child's safety

concern: whether each item is safe to put in front of this child: no way out, and no new fear

about: A suggestion that lands badly with an anxious child is a clinical problem, not a wording one.
These rules are about what the child would read and be asked to do.

## no-way-out

Source: Dr. Walker's rules 1 and 2, as Peter merged them on 2026-08-29.

An item must not contain any of the ways the child avoids facing the feared thing: a safety
behavior they keep, an avoidance they perform, or an escape written into the plan, such as leaving
early or being picked up part way. Doing only the first few minutes of something is fine. A way out
built in is not.

## no-new-fear

Source: docs/plans/ladder-generation.md, "What only a scorer can settle" (Peter, 2026-08-28), and
Peter's decision of 2026-10-16 that when the fear is not known, it must not be guessed.

An item must not introduce a fear the child has not mentioned, or name a feared outcome that is
not in the information given. Sharing a word with what the child said is not enough: a narrower or
different fear than the one recorded is a new fear.

## nothing-invented

Source: docs/plans/ladder-generation.md, decision 4 (Peter, 2026-08-28), and the step-sentence
prompt's rule "Never add anything that is not in the situation or the behavior".

An item must not invent a person, a place or a circumstance that is not in the information given.
Varying a general dimension (where, how long, how many people) is not inventing. Bringing in a
particular person or setting the child never mentioned is.
