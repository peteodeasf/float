# Dr. Walker's rules

concern: whether each item follows the supervising psychologist's written rules for exposure steps

about: These rules come from Dr. Walker's written review of 60 suggested steps on 2026-08-29, recorded
in docs/plans/ladder-generation.md. Where she wrote about a particular child's situation, only the
general rule is given here.

## no-way-out

Source: Dr. Walker's rules 1 and 2, as Peter merged them on 2026-08-29.

An item must not contain any of the ways the child avoids facing the feared thing: a safety
behavior they keep, an avoidance they perform, or an escape written into the plan. In her words:
"the more locations without any SABs the better" (SABs are safety behaviors), and of leaving early:
"Leaving early, etc. does not work ever". Leaving early is not a weaker exposure; it is an escape,
and the child knows it.

This is not a ban on short exposures. Doing the first few minutes of something is fine, because
those minutes contain the feared thing and nothing about them is an escape. The test is not length.
It is whether a way out has been built in.

## vary-the-situation

Source: Dr. Walker's rule 1.

Make a step smaller by changing the situation itself: where it happens, who is there, how much of
it, which part of it. Not by keeping something that makes it feel safer. In her words: "make the
exposures about locations". An item that makes it easier only by keeping a person, an object or a
habit the child relies on breaks this rule.

## symptom-not-target

Source: Dr. Walker's rule 6.

Sometimes the situation recorded is a symptom of a different fear, not the thing to target. Then
breaking it down at all treats the wrong thing. Use this only when the situation as written is
plainly a symptom (a delay, a routine, a mood) and the fear behind it is somewhere else.

## Not grounds for an objection

Source: Dr. Walker's rule 4.

Imagining the situation in detail, and watching other people do it, are real steps. She starts
there. Do not object to an item for being "too easy" or "not a real exposure" because it is one of
these.
